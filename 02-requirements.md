## Requirement notes

**The three refusals that prevent the classic failures.** `409 stale_revision` stops a second device silently overwriting the first. `422 unknown_evidence` stops a step referencing a photo the Hub never confirmed, which is the failure that produces a good-looking record with a missing image. `409 form_unsealed` stops a compile shipping a form nobody finished. Each carries the data needed to act: the current revision, the missing hashes, the blocking form.

**Why submit is a separate act (R14, R15).** A write means saved, not finished. Without an explicit submit, "ready to seal" cannot be stated at form level, and an engineer cannot say "I am done with this step but the second photo is missing" without inventing a status. Flags travel into the manifest so a missing photo stays visible instead of becoming a surprise on site day.

**Why the seal is a person's act (R17, R19, R21).** A compile that sealed would make an early layout check lock the test. Sealing on a person's act means a rehearsal compile cannot freeze anything, and one form's typo cannot re-version the forms that were already right.
